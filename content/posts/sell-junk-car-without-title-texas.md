If you own an old car in your driveway and don’t have the title, you might be asking yourself if you can still junk your car in Texas. Texas currently has an exemption for the purposes of dismantling or scrapping that permits a licensed Used Auto Parts Recycler (UAPR) to buy certain vehicles without being issued a vehicle title.

But this is not to say all **sell junk car without title Texas** scenarios are eligible. The vehicle needs to have specific age and registration-history criteria and also needs to follow TxDMV reporting procedures.

## Can You Sell a Junk Car Without a Title in Texas?

Yes, in certain circumstances. The 2026 rules allow a licensed UAPR to obtain **a vehicle that is 13 years old or older and has not been registered in the past seven years**. The recycler is also required to comply with TxDMV reporting and lien requirements prior to disassembling the vehicle.

This is the most important change for people asking, **can you junk a car without a title in Texas**? Now, the answer is yes, if the vehicle is in the exemption and the buyer is a qualified UAPR. You can also learn more about how to junk a car in Texas and the applicable requirements.

Generally, the recycler should go through the existing process and obtain and surrender acceptable ownership evidence from the vehicle in accordance with Form VTR-340 if the vehicle does not meet these requirements.

### What Is a Used Auto Parts Recycler?

Used Auto Parts Recycler is a business that is authorized to buy vehicles and dismantle them to get usable parts. In this context, UAPR and Used Auto Parts Recycler are the same type of licensed recycler. The 2026 exemption is therefore different from simply selling a vehicle privately to another person. It applies to qualifying vehicles being purchased for dismantling or scrapping.

## 2026 Texas No-Title Junk Car Requirements

When considering **selling a junk car without a title in Texas,** verify the following requirements:

| Requirement | What Texas Requires |
|---|---|
| Vehicle age | At least 13 years old |
| Registration history | Not registered for at least 7 years |
| Buyer | Licensed UAPR |
| TxDMV report | Form VTR-341-2 required |
| Lien issues | Additional requirements may apply |
| Purpose | Dismantling or scrapping |

These requirements come directly from TxDMV’s current UAPR guidance. The age/registration history test is a specific no-title exception that does not apply to a vehicle that fails the test.

For example, a 2009 vehicle that has not been registered since 2018 may potentially meet the age and registration-history requirements in 2026. A newer vehicle or one that was registered more recently would not qualify under this exemption.

## What Happens If Your Car Qualifies?

The recycler has other responsibilities if a qualifying UAPR buys the vehicle without a title. The UAPR is required to fill out and submit the Used Automotive Parts Recycler’s Report for Vehicles Purchased Without Title (**Form VTR-341-2**). The report must be filed within 24 hours of the purchase with TxDMV prior to the dismantling of the vehicle.

The recycler can submit the form in person at the appropriate TxDMV Regional Service Center. A preliminary in-person verification and approval by the UAPR is also available, allowing electronic submission. The recycler will need to submit details including their UAPR number, licensed business name, government photo ID for their agent, and business email address.

After processing, TxDMV provides a stamped copy that the recycler must retain.

### What About a Lien?

A lien can make it more difficult to **sell a Texan scrap car without a title**. As of now, TxDMV has a guideline that a vehicle that has a lien must be released if it is less than 6 years old.

If the recycler cannot obtain the release, additional notice procedures apply. The local county tax assessor-collector must be notified, and a **$25 administrative fee** is required for the county to send notice to the owner and lienholder.

Don’t assume that the fact that a registration is old means that it automatically removes a lien. The TxDMV must be adhered to by the recycler.

## What If the Car Does Not Qualify?

Not every **no-title junk car situation in Texas** falls under the new exemption. If the vehicle is less than 13 years old or has been registered within the past seven years, the UAPR cannot simply use Form VTR-341-2 to bypass the title requirement. Rather, the business needs to receive and submit valid evidence of ownership in accordance with the current procedure (Form **VTR-340**).

This could involve a missing title, acceptable ownership, a lien, or any other TxDMV process depending on the vehicle and ownership history. Form VTR-340 is for use on vehicles being dismantled, scrapped, or destroyed, and documents the evidence of ownership being turned in to TxDMV.

## What Documents Should You Have Ready?

If a title is not on hand, collect all documentation you can to help establish ownership and history for the vehicle. Useful information may include:

- Vehicle identification number (VIN)
- Current or previous registration information
- Driver’s license or other government-issued identification
- Purchase records or bill of sale
- Lien release, if applicable
- Previous title information
- Registration history

The presence of these records may facilitate determination by the recycler as to whether the vehicle is eligible and which process is applicable.

Don’t assume that you can just get a replacement for a **scrap vehicle title** with a bill of sale. The appropriate documentation will vary for the vehicle’s history and/or the required TxDMV procedure.

## How to Junk a No-Title Car in Texas

The first step to selling a junk car without a title in Texas is to determine the age and registration history of the vehicle. Then talk to a licensed UAPR and ask for the VIN to see if the vehicle is eligible for the exemption, which is in 2026.

If it is eligible, the UAPR will provide the necessary Form VTR-341-2 reporting. If not, ask what the process is for ownership before the vehicle can be broken down. A legitimate recycler should be able to explain which Texas procedure applies rather than simply telling you that no title is ever accepted.
