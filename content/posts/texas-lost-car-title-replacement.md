Losing your vehicle title can turn a simple sale into a paperwork headache. Fortunately, if you are the recorded owner of a Texas-titled vehicle, you can request a certified replacement rather than starting the ownership process from scratch.

For Houston-area vehicle owners, the process is handled through the Texas Department of Motor Vehicles (TxDMV). Below, we explain how to replace a Texas **lost car title**, what Form VTR-34 does, how much it costs, and what changes if your vehicle has a lien or is titled under a business or trust.

## Why Your Texas Car Title Matters

A vehicle title is the primary document that establishes recorded ownership. Registration proves that the vehicle is registered, but it does not replace the Certificate of Title when you need to transfer ownership.

This matters when you plan to sell, trade, or otherwise transfer the vehicle. A missing title can delay the transaction because the buyer needs a valid ownership transfer. If you have misplaced the original title, getting a certified copy before advertising the vehicle can make the eventual sale much smoother.

## How to Replace a Lost Car Title in Texas

In Texas, the replacement is called a **Certified Copy of Title.** You can apply either in person at a TxDMV Regional Service Center or by mail.

### Step 1: Complete Form VTR-34

Start with the **Application for a Certified Copy of Title, Form VTR-34**. If the vehicle is titled to an individual, the recorded owner must sign the application and provide a copy of a valid government-issued photo ID. If multiple owners appear on the title record, each owner generally needs to sign and provide identification. Do not use an old registration receipt as a substitute for the required application.

### Step 2: Choose Mail or In-Person Service

You have two basic options:

| Application method | Current TxDMV fee | Key detail |
|---|---|---|
| By mail | $2.00 | Allow at least 15 days |
| In person | $5.45 | Regional Service Center |

For mail applications, TxDMV requires the completed VTR-34, copies of the required photo IDs, the $2 payment, and any additional documentation that applies.

Mail applications are sent to:

**Texas Department of Motor Vehicles, 1601-A Southwest Parkway, Wichita Falls, TX 76302**

TxDMV says to allow **at least 15 days** to receive a Certified Copy when applying by mail.

### Step 3: Visit the Houston Regional Service Center

Houston residents who prefer in-person service can use the TxDMV Regional Service Center at:

**2110 East Governors Circle, Houston, TX 77092**

TxDMV encourages appointments at its Regional Service Centers. Bring the completed application, valid photo ID, and the **$5.45 fee**. For someone preparing to sell a vehicle soon, in-person processing may be more practical than waiting on a mailed application.

## What If There Is Still a Lien?

A lien can change the process. If a lien is recorded on your Texas title record, the lienholder may need to apply for the Certified Copy of Title or provide an **original release of lien**. A photocopy or faxed release is not accepted for this purpose.

This is particularly important if you paid off an auto loan years ago but never received an updated title. A paid loan does not necessarily mean the lien has already been removed from the title record. TxDMV states that after a vehicle loan is paid, the lienholder has **10 days after receiving payment** to release the lien.

Before selling, verify that the title record is clear enough for the ownership transfer you plan to make.

## What If the Vehicle Is Owned by a Business?

Business-owned vehicles have additional identification requirements. When the title is in a business or organization name, the authorized agent generally needs acceptable identification plus documentation connecting that person to the business. TxDMV accepts items such as an authorization letter on company letterhead, an appropriate business card, or employee identification.

This prevents someone without authority from requesting a title copy for a company-owned vehicle. If you are selling a business vehicle in Houston, gather the company’s ownership and authorization documents before visiting the service center.

## What If the Vehicle Is Titled to a Trust?

Trust-owned vehicles require additional documentation because the person applying must demonstrate that they are an authorized trustee. TxDMV may require current trustee identification along with an **Affidavit of Trust, Statement of Fact for a Trust, or original/certified copy of the trust agreement**. A Power of Attorney can add further documentation requirements.

Because trust ownership can involve several documents, it is worth checking the requirements before making a trip to the Houston Regional Service Center.

## What If You Never Had the Title?

A lost title and a vehicle that was never properly titled in your name are two different problems. If you purchased a vehicle but never completed the ownership transfer and no longer have the necessary ownership evidence, you may have to use Texas’s bonded title process instead.

Eligible applicants can submit a Bonded Title Application or related documentation through a TxDMV Regional Service Center. The current administrative fee for the bonded title process is **$15**. Eligibility restrictions apply, including requirements concerning Texas residency and the vehicle’s condition. Do not assume that a bill of sale alone gives you the same rights as a Texas title.

It also makes it easier to provide the buyer with the ownership document needed for the transfer. ## Houston Title Costs You Should Know

The replacement title fee is only one potential cost in a vehicle transaction. For Harris County, the current title fee is **$33**, and the county lists a **6.25% motor-vehicle sales tax** along with other applicable fees for title and registration transactions.

These costs generally apply to the buyer’s title transaction rather than the $2 or $5.45 fee you pay to obtain a Certified Copy. If you are replacing your title specifically because you plan to sell, budget for the replacement fee first and then make sure your eventual sale paperwork is completed correctly.

## Keep Your Replacement Title Safe

Once your Certified Copy arrives, store it somewhere secure rather than keeping it in the vehicle. Avoid leaving the title in your glove compartment. A title contains important ownership information, so keeping it with your other important documents reduces the chance of losing it again.

If you plan to sell soon, do not sign the title until you have a legitimate buyer and are ready to complete the transaction properly.
