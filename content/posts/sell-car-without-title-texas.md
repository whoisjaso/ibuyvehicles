The transfer of a car without title may be more complex than a regular private sale, but it is not necessarily an impossible sale. The answer to this question is different in Texas depending on the owner of the title, the vehicle’s owner, and the status of the title (lien or special).

Paperwork is particularly crucial for those selling in the Houston area. This guide will explain **how to sell a car without a title in Texas**, explain what you should do if you’ve lost the title, and when it may be easier to sell to a vehicle buyer than to deal with the paperwork yourself.

## Is It Possible to Sell a Car Without a Texas Title?

Generally, the Texas Certificate of Title is required for a private sale to be transferred in a normal fashion. TxDMV advises sellers to give the buyer a signed title, date of sale, odometer reading (if required), and a completed Form 130-U with the sales price.

The title is not normally replaced by a bill of sale or registration for the vehicle. But if your title is just missing, there is an easy answer, and that is to order a Certified Copy of Title prior to closing.

Things get more complex when the vehicle is not registered under your name, the lien has not been paid, or the ownership is complicated.

## Lost Your Title? Get a Certified Copy

In case of a lost or destroyed Texas title, the recorded owner can request a certified copy on Form VTR-34. Typically, all recorded owners must sign the application and present a government-issued photo ID.

The current fees are:

- **$5.45** when applying in person
- **$2.00** when applying by mail
- There is a **30-day waiting period** between the issuance of one certified copy of a title and another being requested.

The TxDMV Regional Service Center is at **2110 East Governors Circle in Houston, TX 77092** for residents of Houston. The mailed application goes to the TxDMV Regional Service Center in Wichita Falls.

You may be required to have an original lien release from the lienholder if a lien is still on the vehicle before the title issue can be resolved.

## What If the Car Is Not Titled in Your Name?

This is more complicated than simply losing your title.

You might own a car, but you have not been registered as the owner of the car. There’s a chance that you may have an old bill of sale, registration documents, or other proof of ownership, but this doesn’t necessarily mean you’re able to sign over a clean title.

Texas does have a **bonded title process** for certain situations where acceptable ownership evidence is incomplete or unavailable. Must meet eligibility requirements; may not be junked or determined nonrepairable. If you are unsure whether your paperwork proves ownership, contact TxDMV or your county tax office before advertising the vehicle as a normal title-in-hand sale.

## Main Ways to Sell a Car Without a Title

You will need to choose which option is best depending on why the title is missing.

### Option 1: Replace the Missing Title

This is generally the cleanest route if the vehicle belongs to you and the title was lost. Request the certified copy, receive the replacement, and then proceed with the sale documentation. It provides a private buyer with the paper trail they’d normally need and greatly simplifies the process of passing ownership on.

### Option 2: Sell to a Vehicle Buyer

If you’re looking for someone to purchase your car, you might want to consider a company that buys cars. Depending on the ownership documents and condition of the vehicle, some buyers may be able to purchase vehicles that do not have the original title in their name.

**iBuy Vehicles** can be an option for Houston-area sellers who want to explore selling a vehicle without going through a lengthy private-sale process. However, do not assume every no-title vehicle qualifies. The buyer may need to verify the VIN, ownership records, registration, identification, and other documents before completing the transaction.

### Option 3: Resolve a Complicated Ownership Issue

If it did not pass to the buyer from the previous owner, a lien will remain on the record, or in the case of an inheritance, further documentation might be required at the time of sale. When this happens, both parties can have issues if they rush to sell. Resolve the ownership issue first whenever possible.

## What About Junk Cars Without a Title?

It is also possible to sell a junk car without a title, as long as you can provide proof of legal right to sell the car. The requirements for a junk or damaged car can vary according to its title classification. Nonrepairable and salvage vehicles also have guidelines; be sure not to think that using the same one as for a used car is the same process for every damaged vehicle.

If you are selling a vehicle for parts or scrap, be sure to advise the buyer that the title is not included. A trusted buyer will be able to tell you which documents are appropriate for that vehicle.

## Houston, Texas: Costs and Deadlines to Know

For a standard transfer of ownership in Harris County, the owner is currently paying a title application fee of **$33 plus** Texas motor-vehicle sales tax, which is typically **6.25%.** The other fees listed on the Harris County website will also apply, including a **$2.50** transfer registration fee (if applicable).

For instance, a transaction for a vehicle that is subject to tax is $10,000, and the sales tax would be 6.25% of the sales amount, or $625, before any title, registration, or other fees. Texas also provides its buyers with a **30-day window from the sale date** to register the car. Sellers should provide the Vehicle Transfer Notification to TxDMV within 30 days to limit their liability for the vehicle’s tickets, tolls, etc, after the vehicle is sold.

The seller should keep written records containing the buyer’s information, sale date, vehicle details, and VIN.

## What Documents Should You Have Ready?

Prior to attempting to sell a no-title car, collect as much documentation of ownership as you can.

Useful paperwork may include:

- Valid government-issued photo ID
- Current vehicle registration
- VIN
- Bill of sale or purchase records
- Previous title information
- Lien release, if applicable
- Insurance records
- Other proof of ownership

Having the VIN is particularly important because title and ownership records can be checked against the vehicle identification number. If you lost your title but the car is still listed under your name, getting a certified copy is pretty straightforward. That’s a lot less hassle than trying to prove you own the car from scratch.

## What You Should Not Do

Don’t mess around with legal paperwork if you’re not supposed to. Don’t change, make up, or sign anything that isn’t yours to handle. And don’t tell the buyer something that isn’t true about the car’s title. Always put everything in writing; never just hand the car over without keeping records of the sale.

The TxDMV wants you to keep detailed records and file the Vehicle Transfer Notification after selling. Spend a few extra minutes to document everything; it’ll save you a ton of headaches later.

## Final Thoughts: Selling a No-Title Car in Texas

Not having the title doesn’t mean you can’t sell your car. If you’re the legal owner, just get a Certified Copy of the title; that’s the easiest fix. If things are more tangled or you need cash fast, a professional car buyer might have different solutions.
