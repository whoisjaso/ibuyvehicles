## The Bonded Title Route

A bonded title represents the most common solution for Houston residents. This process involves purchasing a surety bond that protects future buyers against ownership disputes. You pay a bonding company to research your vehicle history and guarantee no hidden claims exist.

The process starts with a VIN inspection through your local DMV office or authorized inspection station. They verify the vehicle identification number matches Texas records and check for theft reports. Once cleared, you apply for the bonded title through the county tax office. The bond typically costs around $100 to $150 depending on your vehicle value.

This route works perfectly for cars you legally own but lack paperwork for. It takes about two to three weeks total. Once issued, you hold a fully legal title that functions exactly like an original. You can then sell your car without title worries following standard transfer procedures.

## Duplicate Title Applications

Sometimes the original title exists somewhere in the system. Perhaps you simply misplaced it, or it never arrived by mail. Texas allows duplicate title applications for these situations. You fill out Form VTR-34 and submit it with a small fee to your county tax assessor-collector.

This option requires you to be the last registered owner in Texas records. If someone else shows as the legal owner, you cannot use this path. The state issues the duplicate to the person listed in their database. Processing usually takes two to four weeks, though expedited options exist for urgent situations.

Keep your driver’s license current and your address updated with the DMV. Mismatched information causes delays. Bring proof of insurance and registration if you have them. These documents support your ownership claim even without the physical title present.

## What Documentation You Actually Need

Even without a title, you must prove the vehicle belongs to you. Gather your driver’s license, vehicle registration if available, and any bill of sale documentation from when you acquired the car. Insurance records showing you as the policyholder help establish ownership history.

If you inherited the vehicle, bring death certificates and estate paperwork. For cars purchased without proper transfer, locate the previous owner’s contact information. Their signature on additional forms might become necessary. Photos of you with the vehicle over time, repair receipts in your name, and any loan payoff documents strengthen your case.
