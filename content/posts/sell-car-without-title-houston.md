## Why Titles Go Missing in Houston

Hurricane season hits Texas hard. Flood waters destroy more than homes, they ruin paperwork too. Many Houston residents lost vehicle titles during Harvey and other major storms. Others inherit cars from family members who never completed proper transfers. Some folks buy project cars intending to restore them, then life gets in the way.

The Texas DMV sees these cases constantly. They built backup systems specifically for this reality. Understanding why your title disappeared helps determine your next step. Each scenario has a slightly different fix, but none of them leave you permanently stuck with a vehicle you cannot sell.

## The Bonded Title Route

A bonded title is an option when you have the vehicle but cannot get the documents needed for a normal title transfer. The process involves purchasing a surety bond that protects anyone who later makes a valid ownership or lien claim against the vehicle.

The process starts with Form VTR-130-SOF, your ownership evidence, photo ID and a $15 fee at a TxDMV Regional Service Center. If the vehicle has no Texas record, a law enforcement VIN inspection (Form VTR-68-A) may also be required. If TxDMV approves, it sends a Notice of Determination with the bond amount, generally 1.5 times the vehicle’s determined value. You buy the bond from a surety company, which sets the premium, and then apply for the title at the county tax office.

This route can work for cars you legally own but lack paperwork for. How long it takes varies, so check current details with TxDMV. Once issued, you have a Texas title and can sell following standard transfer procedures.

## Certified Copy of Title

Sometimes the original title exists somewhere in the system. Perhaps you simply misplaced it, or it never arrived by mail. Texas lets the recorded owner request a Certified Copy of Title for these situations. You fill out Form VTR-34 and apply in person at a TxDMV Regional Service Center ($5.45) or by mail ($2), according to current TxDMV instructions.

This option requires you to be the recorded owner in Texas records. If someone else shows as the legal owner, you cannot use this path. The state issues the certified copy to the person listed in its records. TxDMV says to allow at least 15 days for a mailed copy, and applying in person at the Houston Regional Service Center can be quicker.

Keep your driver’s license current and your address updated with the DMV. Mismatched information causes delays. Bring proof of insurance and registration if you have them. These documents support your ownership claim even without the physical title present.

## Selling an Older Junk Car Without a Title

Texas has a specific no-title route for junk cars. A licensed Used Auto Parts Recycler (UAPR) can buy a vehicle without a title for dismantling or scrap if it is at least 13 years old and has not been registered for at least seven years. The recycler must file Form VTR-341-2 with TxDMV and follow the lien rules. If the car doesn’t meet both tests, the regular ownership process applies, and that usually means a title or other acceptable ownership evidence.

## What Documentation You Actually Need

Even without a title, you must prove the vehicle belongs to you. Gather your driver’s license, vehicle registration if available, and any bill of sale documentation from when you acquired the car. Insurance records showing you as the policyholder help establish ownership history.

If you inherited the vehicle, bring death certificates and estate paperwork. For cars purchased without proper transfer, locate the previous owner’s contact information. Their signature on additional forms might become necessary. Photos of you with the vehicle over time, repair receipts in your name, and any loan payoff documents strengthen your case.

Never attempt to sell a vehicle with active liens without proper release paperwork. The lienholder holds legal claim until the debt clears. Check lien status before you sell to protect everyone involved. Attempting to hide active liens creates legal liability you want to avoid completely.
